Pricing Crypto Event Contracts

A fair-value and market-making framework for Kalshi and Polymarket BTC markets at 15-minute, hourly and daily tenors, plus a test of the pricing core against 6 months of settled Kalshi contracts and Kalshi's own quotes.

Kailen Vyas · November 2026 · Code and data pipeline in a private GitHub repo (access on request)

Summary

The short version

These contracts are short-dated digital options on a published index. Kalshi settles on the 60-second average of CF Benchmarks' BRTI; Polymarket's short markets settle on a Chainlink 60-second TWAP. Fair value is the probability the settlement average finishes above the strike. That probability depends on three things: distance to the strike, how much the index can move before settlement (volatility × √time, reduced by the averaging), and the shape of the move distribution (BTC has fat tails).

Nothing else enters as a "weight" on price. Perp funding, open interest and options IV only matter through the volatility forecast. Drift is set to zero at these horizons.

I built that pricing core in five layers and scored every layer out of sample. The result splits by tenor, and the split tells you where a market maker can compete:

Daily laddersBeats midBrier 0.0925 vs 0.0930. The model carries 80% of the weight in a blend. Only 104 events, so the 95% CI includes zero.
Hourly laddersTies midBrier 0.0923 vs 0.0920. Gap is 0.3 thousandths, and a 50/50 blend beats the mid alone.
15-minute up/downBehind midBrier 0.1653 vs 0.1622, about 4¢ average gap. Index measurement is the likely bottleneck, not the model.
What this means for PinPredict

With free one-minute data, a first-principles model already prices hourly and daily ladders as well as Kalshi's book. That makes it safe to quote around those markets, which is where retail flow concentrates. The 15-minute market rewards measuring the index precisely, sub-second, more than it rewards modeling. So the first infrastructure dollar goes into a live BRTI replica, and 15-minute quoting waits until that replica matches Kalshi's own index stream.

Flowchart 1 · The engine

How one price gets made

Every market runs through the same six stages. Inputs on the left feed each stage. The engine reruns from stage 2 on every index tick; the closed-form pricing takes microseconds, so speed is limited by data and order latency, not math.

Inputs
CoinbaseKrakenBitstampGeminiLMAXBullishCrypto.comKalshi BRTI streamChainlink stream

1Replicate the settlement index

Rebuild BRTI from the seven constituent USD order books every 200 ms, using CF Benchmarks' published method (depth-weighted mid, stale and outlier books dropped). Validate against Kalshi's own cfbenchmarks_value stream. Polymarket gets a separate Chainlink reference, plus a live basis between the two.

S_t = index level now · tested: free 1-min proxy tracks BRTI to 1.2 bp median

Inputs
1-min realized varET time-of-weekDeribit DVOLMacro calendarPerp OI / funding

2Forecast variance to expiry

Log-HAR on de-seasonalized realized variance (last 1h, 24h, 7d) plus DVOL, then multiplied by the expected variance profile of the exact minutes left (the US open runs about 3× normal variance, weekends about 0.67×). Event hours (CPI, FOMC) get a bump. Leverage data (OI, funding, liquidations) can raise jump intensity here; it never shifts the mean.

V = level(t) × Σ season(minute) over [t, T]

Inputs
Window length w = 60sPrints already locked

3Adjust for averaged settlement

The payoff is on a 60-second average, so the final minute adds less variance than a point price would. Before the window, remove 0.658 of the last minute's variance. Inside the window, the locked-in prints define an effective strike K* for the remaining prints.

V_eff = V − 0.658·σ²w · inside: K* = (nK − Σ locked) / m

Inputs
ν ≈ 4.2–4.5 fittedDeribit smile (daily+)

4Choose the shape of the move

Scale-matched Student-t instead of a normal. BTC's standardized 15-minute returns have excess kurtosis near 10. Fat tails pull near-the-money strikes toward 0 or 1 and add value far out. For daily and longer tenors, Deribit's smile can anchor the tails.

P(Ā_T > K) = 1 − F_ν( ln(K/S) / √V_eff )

Inputs
Strike(s)Contract rules

5Map one distribution to every contract

The same terminal distribution prices the whole ladder (P above K), range buckets (CDF differences) and up/down (strike = opening average). Using one distribution keeps the book coherent: digitals are monotone in strike and buckets sum to one, so nobody can arbitrage the ladder against itself.

Inputs
InventoryBinance/Bybit perp leadBook stateFees and rebates

6Quote, protect, hedge

Shade fair value by inventory. Rest at the touch only where expected spread capture plus rebate beats expected pick-off loss. Pull quotes when the perp (which leads spot by 100–300 ms) moves fair value past the edge. Hedge the net delta of the whole book in perps, not contract by contract. Ladder-sweep caps and kill switches sit on top.

reservation = FV − γ·q·Var_h · rest iff spread + rebate − λ·E[pick-off] − inventory cost > 0

Worked example

Pricing one 15-minute contract by hand

BTC 3:00–3:15 window. It is 3:10, so 5 minutes are left. The start (strike) was $80,000; the index is $80,060. Volatility is 45% a year, or 0.062% per minute.

StepMove size (1σ, $)Fair value of YESWhat changed
Normal, point settlement11170.5¢$60 above with a $111 one-sigma move: z = 0.54
+ 60s averaging10471.9¢The final minute counts at about one third, so less variance is left
+ Student-t tails (ν = 4.5)10476.2¢Most moves are small and a few are huge, so a modest lead holds more often
Same, but vol doubles (CPI print)20764.2¢Volatility is the input you forecast. Doubling it moves the price 12¢
Same, but 2 minutes left5888.7¢Less time, less room to fall back below the strike

Hourly example on the same inputs: strike $80,400 with 40 minutes left. A normal gives 14.0¢ and the t gives 10.3¢. If those 40 minutes fall in the 10–11 AM ET hour (about 3× normal variance), fair value rises to 21.8¢. Time of day alone is worth 11¢ on this contract.

Test

How it was tested

Data. Every Kalshi BTC 15-minute and hourly/daily ladder contract from Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, with Kalshi's official settlement value and one-minute bid/ask history. Spot is a free BRTI proxy: the median of Coinbase and Bitstamp one-minute closes, both BRTI constituents. Also hourly Deribit DVOL and Binance spot and perps.

Split. Everything the model learns (variance weights, the time-of-week profile, the tail parameter, a proxy-to-BRTI level offset, blend weights) is fit on contracts closing before 1 May 2026. The scores below are on contracts closing from 1 May to 27 Oct 2026, which the model never saw.

Rows. One row per contract-minute with a real two-sided Kalshi quote (bid ≥ 1¢, ask ≤ 99¢, spread ≤ 10¢). The model sees only data up to that minute. Ladder minutes are sampled every 5 minutes. Test set: 144,255 rows over 11,517 fifteen-minute windows; 257,581 rows over 2,737 hourly events; 534,324 rows over 104 daily events.

Scores. Brier score (mean squared error of the probability against the 0/1 outcome; lower is better), and the average gap to Kalshi's mid in cents. Confidence intervals resample whole events, because minutes within one contract are not independent.

The weights

There are two sets of weights in this model. Inside the variance forecast, a regression decides how much recent realized variance and DVOL count. In the price, those feed one number, σ√τ. Spot, strike and the tail parameter enter through the probability formula, not through weights.

Elasticities: a 10% rise in last-hour variance lifts the next-hour forecast about 5%. Short tenors listen to the last hour; daily tenors lean on DVOL and the 24h level. Realized variance is de-seasonalized before the fit, then re-seasonalized for the minutes being priced.

Results · Findings

What the test showed

Fat tails are the biggest single fix

Adding the Student-t improved every tenor, most of all hourly (gap to mid cut from 1.3 to 0.3 thousandths). On the cheap side of the ladder, 0.5–1.5σ out of the money:

  • The normal model prices these contracts 2–3¢ above the Kalshi mid.
  • They actually paid out about 2¢ below the mid.
  • So a normal model overprices them by 4–5¢. The t-model sits within about 1¢ of realized.

The mid itself overprices these longshots by about 2¢. That matches the favorite-longshot bias documented on Kalshi, and it argues for leaning toward selling those strikes.

Averaging matters in the last few minutes

On 15-minute contracts, the average gap to mid in the final minute falls from 13.3¢ to 8.8¢ with the adjustment, and from 6.6¢ to 5.3¢ with 1–3 minutes left. It is irrelevant beyond about 10 minutes and on hourly and daily, as the math predicts.

The 15-minute gap is likely an index problem

The gap to mid grows as expiry nears: 3.9¢ with 10–15 minutes left, 5.3¢ at 1–3 minutes, 8.8¢ in the final minute. That tracks the contract's sensitivity to the index. With 5 minutes left an at-the-money contract moves about 3¢ per basis point of BTC, and the free one-minute proxy misses BRTI by 1.2 bp at the median (5.8 bp at p95). The market sees the index sub-second; this test saw one-minute closes from two of seven exchanges. Inference The fix is stage 1, not a better model.

What didn't help, or wasn't tested

  • The time-of-week profile beat plain EWMA on hourly and daily but not on 15-minute. A fast EWMA already reflects the current regime over a 15-minute window.
  • DVOL is a 30-day vol. It carries real weight for dailies and adds almost nothing to 15-minute pricing.
  • Funding, open interest, sentiment: not in this model. An earlier check found funding has no directional signal at these horizons (correlation −0.02 with the next 8h return). OI and liquidations are candidates for the jump-intensity input, still to test.
  • Not tested yet: macro-event variance bumps, Polymarket, the partial-average strike inside the final minute (needs 1-second data), and simulated market-making P&L.

Market making

From a fair value to a quote

Where the money comes from

  • Spread capture against retail flow, concentrated in hourly and daily markets. Bots are 55–62% of 15-minute volume but about 31% of daily volume.
  • Kalshi LP rewards (auctioned slots). Polymarket's 20% maker fee rebate.
  • The small vol premium the book carries, and the longshot overpricing.
  • Netting one book across every strike and tenor on the same index. Opposite positions hedge each other, so the book can quote tighter than single-market bots.

Where it gets lost

  • Pick-off. At 15 minutes an at-the-money digital moves about 1.5¢ per bp of BTC, and the taker fee shields only about 1.2 bp of movement. In the final minute it is 5.8¢ per bp. Cancel latency is the defense.
  • Ladder sweeps. One BTC gap fills every strike on the way, so aggregate resting size per direction needs a cap.
  • Hedging. Per-contract perp hedges cost 2–7¢ at 15 minutes against under 1¢ of edge. Hedge the net book delta in bands instead.

Quote rules

  • Inventory skew in binary units: reservation = FV − γ·q·Var_h, where Var_h shrinks to p(1−p) at settlement.
  • The book is tick-bound (one tick wide about 92% of the time), so the decision is whether to rest at the touch and how much, not how wide.
  • Reprice on every perp tick. Pull when the fair-value move exceeds the half-spread plus fee.
  • Widen or step out for CPI, FOMC, thin overnight hours and alt series.

Risk limits and kill switches

  • Each expiry's book is a payoff vector over settlement values. Limit the exact worst-case loss per expiry.
  • Per-asset delta, a joint BTC/ETH shock grid (−3/−5/−8% in one minute), Kalshi's $25k-per-strike accountability.
  • Kill on stale or divergent index feeds, clock drift, realized vol above k× the model, drawdown, and Kalshi order-group fill triggers. Polymarket heartbeat dead-man switch.

Rollout, each phase gated on a measured number

Phase 1 · Shadow

Run the full engine live on recorded feeds and the real books with no orders. Rebuild the BRTI replica and measure it against Kalshi's stream.

Gate: replica residual under 0.5 bp. Model ties mid on hourly and daily, live.

Phase 2 · Hourly and daily

Quote small size where the model already ties or beats the book. Track markouts per fill.

Gate: positive spread capture net of markouts over enough expiries to matter.

Phase 3 · 15-minute

Turn on 15-minute quoting with the sub-second replica, perp-lead pulls and the in-window partial-average strike.

Gate: 15-minute model gap to mid under 1¢ in shadow.

Phase 4 · LP and Polymarket

Bid for Kalshi LP slots. Add Polymarket with the Chainlink and Binance references and a basis model.

General framework

Crypto on prediction markets, in general

  1. Read the settlement rule first. Which index, which exchange, average or point print, tie rule, timezone. Most mispricings on these venues come from getting this wrong.
  2. Replicate that index exactly, at the speed the market reads it.
  3. Forecast variance for the exact minutes left, not a generic annual vol. Time of day and scheduled events move it by 3×.
  4. Use a fat-tailed distribution and price every contract on the same index from it.
  5. Set drift to zero. Funding, OI and sentiment belong in the variance or jump inputs, if they earn a place at all.
  6. Benchmark against the venue's own mid, out of sample. Matching it is the requirement to quote; beating it is a bonus.
  7. Make the money in market making: spread, rebates, LP rewards and netting, protected by speed and limits.

The same recipe covers ETH, SOL and other assets on Kalshi, ForecastEx's matching 15-minute contract, and Polymarket US. Each needs its own index check and parameters.
