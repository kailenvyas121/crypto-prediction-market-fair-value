Pricing Crypto Event Contracts

A fair-value and market-making framework for Kalshi and Polymarket crypto markets at 15-minute, hourly, daily and longer tenors, tested out of sample against a year of Kalshi data: settled outcomes, Kalshi's own quotes, and 7.8 million real fills.

Kailen Vyas · November 2026 · Code, data pipeline and full write-up: github.com/kailenvyas121/pinpredict-crypto-pricer

Summary

The short version

A crypto prediction contract is a digital option on a published index. Kalshi settles on the 60-second average of CF Benchmarks' BRTI; Polymarket's short markets settle on Chainlink or Binance prices. Fair value is the probability that the settlement value lands on the paying side. It depends on three things: distance to the strike, how far the index can move before settlement (volatility × √time, reduced by the averaging), and how fat the tails are.

Bitcoin has no cash flows, so over minutes to days its fair value is entirely a question of the distribution of moves. Every input that matters for that distribution is public, 24/7 market data. That makes this a numbers problem. It does not make it easy: BTC runs about 45% volatility with fat tails, and news hits it as jumps.

I built the pricer in layers, scored every layer on data it never saw, and then asked the question a market maker cares about: do makers make money on these contracts, and can the model tell good fills from bad ones?

What this means for PinPredict

Makers on Kalshi's BTC markets are paid, the model already prices hourly and daily contracts as well as the book, and it roughly triples the hourly maker's edge by telling it where to stand. Start on hourly and daily, where the latency bar is lowest and retail flow concentrates. Bring in 15-minute markets once a sub-second index replica and volatility read close the remaining gap. Use positioning data and the news feed for risk (widening, pulling, sizing), not for fair value. Our data shows they don't forecast price; they describe danger.

Flowchart 1 · The engine

How one price gets made

Every market runs through the same six stages. The engine reruns on every index tick; the pricing math takes microseconds, so speed is limited by data and order latency.

Inputs
CoinbaseKrakenBitstampGeminiLMAXBullishCrypto.comKalshi BRTI streamChainlink

1Replicate the settlement index

Rebuild BRTI from the seven constituent USD order books every 200 ms with CF Benchmarks' method. Validate against Kalshi's live BRTI stream. Polymarket gets its own Chainlink or Binance reference and a basis to BRTI.

Tested: a free two-exchange proxy tracks the settlement to 1.2 bp median. Pricing off Binance USDT instead misses the market by 21¢ on 15-minute contracts

Inputs
Realized var 1h/24h/7dET time of weekDeribit DVOLMacro calendar

2Forecast variance for the exact minutes left

Log-HAR on realized variance plus DVOL, times the variance profile of the minutes left (US open ~3× average, weekends 0.67×), plus release bumps (first 5 minutes after CPI, payrolls or FOMC: 7.5× forecast).

V = level(t) × Σ season(minute) over [t, T] + event bumps

Inputs
60 one-second printsPrints already locked

3Adjust for averaged settlement

The final minute counts at about a third of a point print. Remove 0.658 of its variance; inside the window, locked prints define an effective strike.

Tested: final-minute gap to mid 13.3¢ → 8.8¢

Inputs
ν ≈ 4.2–4.5Deribit smile (daily+)

4Use fat tails

Scale-matched Student-t. This was the largest single improvement. A normal overprices cheap strikes by 4–5¢ vs outcomes; the t is within about 1–1.5¢.

P(Ā_T > K) = 1 − F_ν( ln(K/S) / √V_eff )

Inputs
StrikesContract rules

5Price every contract from one distribution

Ladders, buckets and up/down come from the same distribution, so prices fall with strike and buckets sum to one. Nobody can arbitrage the quotes against each other.

Inputs
InventoryPerp ticksNews alertsLiquidations / OI

6Quote, protect, hedge

Shade for inventory; rest where the model edge beats expected pick-off loss. Pull when the perp moves (it leads spot by 100–300 ms) or a news alert fires. Hedge the book's net delta in perps. Ladder-sweep caps and kill switches on top.

Does the model add to the mid?

Blend weight on the model, fit on training data: 0.26 (15-min), 0.51 (hourly), 0.80 (daily). Out of sample the blend beats the mid alone on hourly (0.09191 vs 0.09200) and daily (0.09246 vs 0.09300), so the model carries information the book does not.

Why 15-minute trails: inverting each quote for the index level the market must be using gives a 0.9 bp median gap to our proxy (the proxy's known error size), correlated 0.34 with the gap implied by the hourly ladder at the same minute. The gap also grows with strike distance, which points to the market reading short-term volatility faster. Sub-second index replication is necessary for 15-minute markets, and a second-by-second volatility read is the other half.

Do makers make money, and does the model help?

The model sorts fills

On hourly, maker P&L rises with the model's edge: < −5¢: −1.58¢, −5 to −2¢: −0.79¢, −2 to 0¢: +0.28¢, 0 to 2¢: +0.69¢, 2 to 5¢: +1.04¢, > 5¢: +2.74¢. A maker who stands only where the model sees edge earns about three times the average maker per contract.

Where the money is

  • Retail-sized hourly fills (≤10 contracts) pay makers +1.10¢ per contract.
  • On 15-minute contracts the final minute is roughly break-even for makers (+0.10¢), with a −0.73¢ one-minute markout: that is where pick-off lives.
  • Cheap contracts on both sides are overpriced by about 2¢ on 6,470 hourly and daily events: a longshot premium makers collect.

Limits: observed fills went to whichever makers were at the front of the queue; a new entrant gets a share and may be adversely selected more. The model-filtered numbers assume those fills would have been ours, and the daily filter result (20 events) is noise. A queue-position replay on recorded Kalshi books is the next test.

Test 3 · Spot and derivatives signals

What perps, open interest and funding tell you

Perps lead spot, briefly

Binance perp vs spot at 100 ms: correlation 0.18 when the perp moves first vs 0.09 when spot does; the last second of the perp explains 1.7% of the next second of spot. Worthless for a 15-minute fair value, valuable for pulling stale quotes.

Positioning doesn't forecast

OI change, OI level, funding, basis, long/short ratios and taker flow: out-of-sample R² for BTC's next 1h / 4h / 24h return is −0.4% / −2.0% / −10.8%. Added to the volatility forecast (which explains 63% of next-hour log variance), they add nothing. A 0.9% "signal" disappeared once OI snapshots were time-stamped conservatively.

The OI story describes; it doesn't predict

Price up/down × OI up/down ("new longs", "short covering", "new shorts", "long liquidation"): no quadrant predicted the next 4 hours (largest |t| = 1.35). Crowded longs did not have fatter downside tails in this sample.

Case: 10 Oct 2025

Before the record (~$19B reported) liquidation cascade, OI was +0.8σ and funding normal. A tariff post triggered it; in the crash hour realized volatility was 2.8× the forecast and OI fell 18.6%. News triggers, leverage amplifies, and live liquidation data identifies the cascade while it happens. That makes it a kill-switch input.

One-factor market: ETH β ≈ 1.2 to BTC (R² 0.74–0.82), SOL β ≈ 1.25 (R² 0.65–0.76); both moved with BTC in all of its top-1% hours. Price alt contracts as BTC variance × β² plus a residual, and net them in one book.

Sentiment and news

X is crypto's newswire

A. News defense (all tenors)

Exchange hacks, ETF decisions and policy posts land on X first. A curated X stream plus a rules-and-LLM classifier pulls or widens quotes within about a second. At 15 minutes a 1% BTC move shifts near-money contracts by tens of cents. The January 2024 fake SEC ETF post (BTC +$1k then −$2k in minutes) is why defense widens on unverified headlines instead of taking a side.

B. Crowd error (longer tenors)

Cheap Kalshi contracts are overpriced by about 2¢ on both sides (6,470 events, intervals exclude zero). Downside longshots are most overpriced after strong rallies (3.6¢) and fairly priced after declines. That is suggestive, not established. For weekly-to-yearly markets the comparison becomes the options-implied fair value, and the trade is to sell the rich side and hedge it in options.

C. Direction: not supported

Retail attention (Wikipedia pageviews) did not improve next-day volatility forecasts, and its link to next-week returns was weak (R² 1%). Drift stays at zero.

Scheduled releases are in the model

First 5 minutes after CPI or FOMC: about 21× forecast variance; payrolls 7.6×. A bump fit on training releases improved Brier on test-period hourly contracts spanning a release (0.1265 vs 0.1275 for the mid), with too few releases to call it significant yet.

Roadmap

How the live system works and gets built

One loop at three speeds. Every tick: sense (exchange books, perps, venue books, news) → rebuild the index → price every contract → quote → guard (limits, pulls, kill switches) → execute. Every day: record everything, refit the volatility weights, seasonality, tails, basis and event bumps, score against the mid and markouts, promote only what holds out of sample. Every week: review P&L by tenor, venue and hour; adjust limits and which markets to quote.

1 · Live shadow

Feeds from the 7 BRTI exchanges, Kalshi BRTI stream, perps, Deribit; pricer live with no orders.

Gate: replica within 0.5 bp of Kalshi's BRTI; live model matches the backtest.

2 · Hourly and daily

Quote engine, risk book, perp hedger, kill switches, news defense v1. Small size.

Gate: positive spread capture net of markouts over many expiries.

3 · 15-minute

Sub-second index and volatility read, perp-lead pulls, in-window averaging math.

Gate: gap to mid under 1¢ in shadow; pick-off below spread in replay.

4 · Long-dated, Polymarket, LP

Options-implied pricing and hedging; Chainlink and Binance replicas; bid for Kalshi LP slots.

Gate: hedged relative value and LP economics clear their costs.

Limits

What is not proven yet

  • 15-minute pricing needs sub-second data from all seven BRTI exchanges; the test used one-minute data from two.
  • Maker P&L is measured on fills that happened, not on a queue-position replay of our own quotes.
  • Polymarket, weekly-to-yearly contracts, touch markets and the news feed are designed but not tested; each needs data listed in the repo's results doc.
  • The daily accuracy result rests on 104 events; the release-bump result on 18 event-hours.
